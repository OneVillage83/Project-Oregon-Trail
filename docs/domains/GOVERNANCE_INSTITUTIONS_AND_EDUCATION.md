# Governance, institutions, organizations and education

Version 0.2 | Owner: social/economy-systems lead | Requirement PR-017

## Representation

An institution has stable identity, charter/rule version, territory/service scope, assets, budget, roles, membership, records and active policies. A role grants bounded authority to an actor; it is not permanent ownership of the institution. Offices and property persist when individuals retire or die.

Institution types can include schools, clinics, businesses, guilds, newspapers, courts and governing bodies. These are shared organizational structures plus domain-specific services, not separate simulated worlds.

## Policy contract

A directive has issuer/authority, goal predicate, geographic/recipient scope, allowed resources, spending limits, priorities, schedule, review horizon, termination and interruption conditions. It creates bounded work orders for authorized staff. 'Build schools in larger towns' requires suitable sites, financing, designs, labor and supplies; it cannot spawn buildings.

Institutional actions use shared plans and accounting. Policies can conflict over resources; a documented priority/approval mechanism resolves them. A role change revokes or revalidates pending authority. Records distinguish proposal, adoption, implementation and evaluated outcome.

## Budgets and public works

Revenue, taxes, donations, wages, maintenance, procurement and borrowing are explicit transactions/obligations. A public project has ownership, procurement, permits, work orders and inspections. Insufficient funds or supplies delay work rather than trigger hidden subsidies.

Government/institution behavior is a versioned setting abstraction, not a claim about current real politics or law. Historical portrayals require research and review. Player authority must derive from an actual role, agreement or social process.

## Education and knowledge

Teaching requires a teacher/source with relevant knowledge, a learner, time, access, materials/facilities and a curriculum/practice process. Completion improves capabilities according to validated learning rules; reading a label cannot instantly grant mastery. Institutions retain curricula, records and capability even when individual staff change, subject to resource and transmission rules.

Research distinguishes hypothesis, experiment, result, replication/acceptance and dissemination. Discovery requires supported processes; a model-generated idea does not automatically unlock a technology. Information can be lost locally, poorly transmitted or unavailable because equipment/funding is missing.

## Organizational scale

Aggregate routine service loads where safe while pinning named participants, contracts, influential records and player commitments. Keep institutional knowledge separate from every member's personal knowledge. A newspaper selects publishable acquired claims and bears provenance/credibility; publication does not turn those claims into world truth.

## Acceptance

SC-INSTITUTION covers delegated spending caps, role revocation, budget shortfall, competing policies and continuity after leader death. SC-EDUCATION covers learner progression, unavailable expertise, record persistence and technology diffusion through actual channels. SC-TOWN tests a public project from proposal through maintenance without invented labor or material.
