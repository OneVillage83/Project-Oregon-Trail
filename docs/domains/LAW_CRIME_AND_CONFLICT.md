# Law, crime, investigation and conflict

Version 0.2 | Owner: social-systems/gameplay lead | Requirements PR-009/016

## Domain boundaries

Historical truth, observed evidence, accusations and institutional decisions are separate. Law owns jurisdiction, rule definitions, authority, complaints, proceedings, sanctions and appeals. Conflict owns declared aggressive/defensive intentions and their tactical resolution. Health owns injuries; Property owns transfers; Knowledge owns witnesses and beliefs. No law rule can rewrite a past event to make a verdict true.

Laws in this game are versioned setting rules, not real legal advice. Exact historical/legal content requires reviewed setting research. Do not assume that one universal modern legal system applies across all eras and communities.

## Crime and evidence

A potentially unlawful action still uses the shared operation system and can succeed or fail physically. The crime classification follows relevant jurisdiction, conduct and authority. Discovery depends on witnesses, traces and communication. A global wanted flag must not instantly inform every distant NPC.

A case records alleged events, applicable rules, acquired evidence, witnesses, chain of custody, contradictions, investigator actions and institutional findings. The underlying causal event is committed before investigation. Evidence can decay, be moved, forged or misinterpreted only through supported causal operations; model narration cannot manufacture it.

A player accusation creates a claim and social/institutional response, not proof. False accusations, uncertainty, unsolved cases and differing institutional competence are valid outcomes. A confession is a committed speech act with belief/deception state, not automatically a guarantee of historical truth.

## Conflict model

Start with time-based abstract/tactical resolution consistent with the rest of the simulation, not a separate action-game physics implementation. Actions consume actor time, position, stamina, equipment condition and actual ammunition where applicable. Intent can include attack, defend, flee, restrain, negotiate or surrender. Outcomes use bounded game abstractions for reach, visibility, cover, preparedness and capability.

These are game mechanics, not instructions for real violence. Avoid operational weapon-building or hazardous-content recipes. Level of detail, presentation, gore and rating are product/content decisions. Nonviolent options are genuine supported actions, not cosmetic buttons that always fail.

## Consequences

Injury affects capability through the health system. Damage/loss affects property. Witness reports propagate through channels. Detention changes custody and task availability through authorized institutional operations; it does not delete a character. Restitution, fines, confiscation or reputation effects need explicit events and resource accounting.

Player and NPC actions share authority, equipment, knowledge and execution rules. A sheriff cannot know a hidden culprit because their role label says sheriff. An NPC's fear may influence decisions but does not bypass consent/property boundaries automatically.

## Persistence and acceptance

Persist jurisdiction/rule versions, case records, custody, warrants/notifications, evidence provenance and interrupted conflict plans. Reload cannot reroll the culprit or erase a sanction without a corresponding event.

SC-CASE tests causal traces, contamination and unsupported accusations. SC-LAW tests jurisdiction, delayed reports, authority and wrongful findings. SC-CONFLICT tests fleeing, surrender, equipment/resource use, injury, witness knowledge and actor loss during plans. Public UI and accessible text must never reveal developer-only case truth.
