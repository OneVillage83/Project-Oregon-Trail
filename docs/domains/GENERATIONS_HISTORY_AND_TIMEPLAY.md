# Generations, persistent history and long-duration play

Version 0.2 | Owner: gameplay/history lead | Requirements PR-008/019/031

## Continuity model

A campaign outlives an individual character. Preserve world, settlement, parcel, family, institution and historically significant object identities. Retirement/death can transfer player focus to a valid party member, heir or institutional role. These are working continuity mechanisms requiring usability and content review, not silently approved historical inheritance rules.

Separate kinship, household membership, legal succession, property ownership and player-control eligibility. Family relation alone does not automatically grant all assets or institutional authority. Succession rules are versioned setting content. Unresolved obligations, guardianship or contested titles remain explicit state.

## Lifecycle transaction

A death/retirement event updates character availability; interrupts or delegates tasks under existing authority; releases or transfers reservations; updates custody/property through approved rules; notifies relevant observers/channels; offers valid continuation options; and records history. No asset or pending commitment disappears because its previous owner is no longer active.

Named deceased actors remain referencable in biographies, evidence and family history. Reusing their IDs for new NPCs is forbidden. Institutions can continue with vacant offices or successors rather than being deleted.

## Long-time interaction

Pause/advance is the default. Fast-forward executes standing orders and scheduled world changes up to a horizon, stopping for user-selected meaningful events: severe danger, budget breach, major death, contract decision, project completion or opportunity needing consent. Less important repeated results are grouped into exact summaries with drilldown.

A policy defines what may proceed unattended, resource limits, authority, review times and fail-safe behavior. Time compression changes presentation and scheduler work granularity under the saved fidelity policy, not causal rules. Application closure does not advance time by default. No system-clock jump can age a campaign unexpectedly.

## History representation

Keep significant event IDs, actors, locations, dates, causes, outcomes, names and source provenance. Derive biographies, newspapers, monuments, institutional records and family narratives from permitted records. A historical account may be biased or mistaken; record its authorship/beliefs separately from authoritative past events.

Not every routine meal needs permanent prose. Keep permanent consequential history and a bounded detailed replay window under the persistence policy. Compaction preserves referenced obligations, evidence, lineage and construction history. A generated summary cannot erase or replace the facts it summarizes.

## Meaningful progression

Technological/settlement development should be observable through real investments, shortages, public works and changing daily choices. A player must have practical ways to see decades pass without issuing a command every hour. Test retirement, delegation, travel revisits, family/institution continuation and historical summaries as gameplay—not only accelerated debug scripts.

Historical names and reputation follow actual communication. A place can remember a bridge builder while a remote character has never heard of them. Fame does not imply universal accurate knowledge.

## Acceptance

SC-CONTINUITY covers death during a plan, succession with debt/property, institutional leadership change, revisiting a replaced structure, record compaction and long-duration reload. SC-TIMEPLAY tests interruption guarantees, unattended spending limits, grouped summaries and player comprehension. A century-long automated run without corruption is necessary technical evidence but insufficient evidence of enjoyable play.
