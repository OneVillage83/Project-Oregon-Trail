# Resource planning and release governance

Version 0.2 | Owner: product/release lead

## Responsibilities

Cover product/game direction, simulation/native engineering, client/UX, language evaluation, technical art/content/tools, QA/build/reliability and editorial/licensing review. A small team may combine roles. These are responsibilities, not claims of hired staff.

Prioritize specialist review of simulation, native build/persistence and determinism early. Art/UI support grows around stable state manifests. Language evaluation needs an owner who distinguishes semantic mistakes from fluent prose. QA begins with contracts and fixtures.

## Estimation

Use measured weekly capacity after G-2 rather than invented dates. Estimate tested operations, process/prototype families, asset states, scenarios, migrations and device configurations. Include iteration, review, defects, tools, research, localization, packaging and support.

Record optimistic/expected/adverse work totals and assumptions per gate. Convert them to dates only with an identified team and available capacity. Offline inference avoids mandatory per-command cloud spending, not production or maintenance costs.

## Costs

Track engineering/review, art/audio rights, testing devices, artifact distribution, model evaluation or justified tuning, specialist review, platform/signing requirements, localization, support and contingency. Naming a subsystem does not authorize a purchase or subscription. No recurring inference service is required for baseline play.

## Release layers

Internal proofs validate architecture. A demo needs a coherent loop, tutorial, save integrity and explicit feature/era boundaries. Early access or a smaller paid release is a separate scope decision, not an automatic reduction of the generational multi-era destination. Final release requires approved content/capability and quality matrices.

Commercial model, price, final name, setting, rating and launch era ceiling remain open. Premium purchase is a planning possibility, not an approved price or revenue projection.

## Approval

Product reviews scope/customer promises; technical lead reviews architecture/reproducibility; QA reviews evidence/defects; content/release reviewers check rights, notices, setting material and packaging. Roles may be combined but responsibilities remain explicit.

A documentation upload to a draft branch does not merge main or approve every open decision. Preserve PR review, evidence reports and timestamped history. When constrained, reduce internal proof content quantity rather than silently abandoning required architectural boundaries. Failed stack/model approaches receive a bounded alternative test and recorded decision.
