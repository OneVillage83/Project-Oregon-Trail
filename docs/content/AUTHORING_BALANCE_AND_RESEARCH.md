# Content authoring, balancing and research workflow

Version 0.2 | Owner: content/tools/QA leads

## Production loop

Define the gameplay question and requirement; identify reusable operations/components; write a versioned content definition; attach positive/negative scenarios; compile/validate; run headless simulations; inspect observer-safe presentation; conduct playtests; review research/provenance; then approve the pack. Do not create large quantities of untested recipes, diseases, technologies or quests merely to increase content counts.

A content change includes its author/reviewer, source provenance, rule assumptions, compatible schema/capability versions, expected outcomes and the cases where the abstraction is not valid. Content can be complete for a fixture without being approved for a shipped historical setting.

## Authoring tools

The prototype editor displays units, required properties and dependent operations. The process editor checks inputs, stages, reservations, outputs, waste and interruptions. Scene tools validate geometry, anchors, object identities and required states. Technology tools show knowledge/capacity/adoption dependencies. Situation tools verify requester authority, funds, communication and underlying world needs.

Knowledge/evidence inspectors show truth and observer views separately in developer mode. Balance dashboards report authoritative quantities and outcomes, not generated narrative sentiment. Editors and runtime share schemas/validators; an editor-only success path is prohibited.

## Balance data and experiments

Keep coefficients, thresholds, progression rates, price responses, needs, decay, adoption and notification priorities in versioned data with bounds and rationale. Record the hypothesis before changing them, identify affected requirements/scenarios and compare baseline versus candidate under identical seeds and normalized commands.

Use distributions, not only an average run. Track deaths and causes, unmet needs, available alternatives, stockouts, wealth concentration, travel/production times, resource depletion, skill growth, institution viability and technology deadlocks. Inspect unintended positive feedback and exploitable loops. Never correct bad balance by silently creating resources or granting hidden knowledge.

Separate mechanical fairness, player comprehension, difficulty and historical fidelity. A numerically realistic duration can still be tedious without good time controls. A balanced fictional disease abstraction is not a clinical claim. Explain tradeoffs and require explicit approval where they change the product's promise.

## Research and editorial approval

Historical geography, cultures, institutions, medicine and technology need appropriate primary or authoritative sources and specialist review where consequential. Record the claim, source, date/edition, uncertainty and how it is translated into a bounded game mechanic. Do not portray generated fiction as historical evidence or derive character ability from demographic stereotypes.

Use fictional named fixtures until the setting is approved. Clinical and engineering details remain game abstractions; avoid presenting gameplay as real-world instructions. Sensitive content, rating and accessibility options are reviewed against actual shipped material.

## Scope and change control

A pack manifest lists required capabilities, asset/localization coverage, license notices and save implications. Cosmetic, balance and rules changes are classified separately. Changes to future simulation rules update compatibility policy and do not retroactively rewrite retained historical events.

Log useful failed experiments, rejected values and reversions. Preserve input seeds and reports so later developers do not repeat dead ends. A large generated content set must still pass the same validation and review as hand-authored work.

## Acceptance

SC-CONTENT validates compiler errors and coverage. SC-REGIONAL and SC-TECH reveal economic/technological deadlocks. SC-ONBOARDING and SC-TIMEPLAY evaluate comprehension and pacing. A production pack needs both automated and human evidence. B-028 starts with small authoring tools early, then grows alongside domain implementation.
